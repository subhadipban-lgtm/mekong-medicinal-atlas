Evidence grading rubric

Every use record, pharmacology record and species carries an evidence grade and a source identifier. A row citing several sources takes the best grade among them; a species takes the best grade of its rows. Grade A is given only when the article is confirmed — by DOI, by PMID or PMC id, or by a Crossref match on author, year and journal or species — or when the citation is a complete journal reference.

Grade A was assigned to 296 taxa on the basis of a median of 2 independent sources per taxon (range 1–12). 173 taxa are supported by a single source and are flagged in the provenance file as low-redundancy.

68 citations were found to carry an error (a wrong DOI or PMID, or a misspelled journal or author) while grading; each is listed in the repository's QA report.

Occurrence records

Records were harvested from the GBIF occurrence search API on 2026-11-24 for each species-level atlas taxon in the six basin countries (georeferenced, without geospatial issues): 153,800 records from 147 GBIF datasets. A record is published only if it has an open licence, a recognised basis of record and verifiable metadata (153,798 published; 2 excluded). Of the published records, 116,092 are specimen-based and 36,652 are observations. Each record was tested against the Mekong basin boundary:

Coordinate generalisation policy

Exact localities of threatened medicinal plants can lead collectors to wild populations, so the atlas generalises the coordinates of sensitive species: those with an IUCN category of Critically Endangered, Endangered or Vulnerable (as recorded in the project workbook or attached by GBIF) or listed in CITES Appendix I or II.

  • Critically Endangered — coordinates rounded to 0.1 decimal degrees (about 11 km).
  • Endangered, Vulnerable, or CITES Appendix I or II — coordinates rounded to 0.01 decimal degrees (about 1.1 km).
  • For every generalised record the locality text is removed and the coordinate uncertainty is raised to at least the rounding cell; the record states this in dataGeneralizations and informationWithheld.
  • All other records keep the precision supplied by GBIF, capped at five decimals.

Geospatial layers

  • Mekong basin boundary — 96 catchment polygons dissolved into one boundary, EPSG:4326, from the Zenodo deposit of Tiwari et al. (doi:10.5281/zenodo.7622998, CC-BY 4.0), derived from an SRTM basin mask by Dang et al. 2022 (Sci Total Environ 831:154833). The boundary is also the test surface that classifies every record as inside or outside the basin.
  • Mekong River centreline — rendered from the WWF-Laos feature service on ArcGIS Online, which states no licence, so it is loaded from the publisher at run time and not redistributed (861 vertices when last checked). The committed fallback is the Natural Earth 1:10m centreline (public domain, 1514 vertices).
  • Country outlines — Natural Earth 1:110m (public domain), unfilled: the atlas reports basin presence and does not shade countries.
  • Basemap — CartoDB Positron raster tiles (© CARTO; © OpenStreetMap contributors).

Macroecology (global residual framework)

The atlas reproduces the residual analysis of a global study of 369 botanical countries1 on the published data for the seven botanical countries that contain the Mekong basin, and extends it to admin-1 (province) scale as an internal under-documentation screen. Model: ordinary least squares, medicinal richness ~ vascular richness, over all botanical countries except Antarctica and Oceania (the authors' exclusion); significance at |residual| > 1.96 SD per their residual_histogram.R. Reproduction is validated against the residuals column shipped in their repository (r = 0.998161). The sub-national extension fits the same model on GBIF vascular species counts per province against the number of atlas medicinal taxa with occurrence records in that province; provinces with significantly negative residuals in areas of long human occupancy (reused from the authors' country-level column) are flagged as fieldwork priorities — the documented difference from the global model is stated on the macroecology page and in the manuscript. Human occupancy is reused at its published resolution, never re-derived; language diversity (Glottolog points aggregated to admin-1) is mapped as context only, since the global model found only indirect climate-mediated effects.

¹ Shrestha et al., The human fingerprint of medicinal plant species diversity, Current Biology (2025), doi:10.1016/j.cub.2025.09.050.

Evidence engine (pilot)

A parallel evidence layer maps publications, compounds and — from its Milestone 2 — compound–target–pathway networks for atlas taxa. Pilot scope: the 20 taxa with the most pharmacology records. Publications come from Europe PMC (primary) and OpenAlex (secondary), searched by accepted name and synonyms with a fixed query template; every query is recorded in the PRISMA search log (data/evidence/search_log.csv). Evidence tiers are assigned by an automated keyword rubric (auto-rubric-v1) on title and abstract; rows the rubric cannot place are flagged needs_review for a human queue — machine classification is never presented as evidence strength. Compounds are seeded from the pharmacology table and resolved against PubChem (CID, InChIKey, SMILES); unresolved names are flagged rather than guessed. Milestone-1 metadata is a citation-sorted slice (top 100 per query), so tier shares are not yet corpus-wide; full pagination arrives with the scheduled living review. Target promotion (Milestone 2) uses a two-independent-lines rule: prediction in two engines, or prediction plus literature-mined support.

Limitations

  • Ethnobotanical, pharmacological and trade content comes from a project workbook that cites primary literature per row; the workbook itself is not redistributed.
  • Local names in Khmer, Lao, Thai, Vietnamese, Burmese and Chinese are not yet recorded: the Tier-2 reference books and the Lancang-Mekong Traditional Medicine Resources Database are access pending.
  • Occurrence records are not re-identified and carry the names GBIF supplies; 4 workbook entries name only a genus and have no occurrence data.
  • The GBIF harvest is an API snapshot without a download DOI; a DOI-citable GBIF download should replace it at release.
  • IUCN categories come from the workbook trade sheet and from GBIF, not from the IUCN API.
  • The map shows records, and a record is not evidence that a plant is used medicinally at that place.

Quality-assurance results and the conflict report (taxonomic synonyms, country-versus-basin discrepancies, citation errors, coordinate outliers) are in the repository under docs/.
