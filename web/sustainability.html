Macroecology — medicinal plant diversity at Mekong scale

Two datasets, different coverage

Atlas inventory: 333 curated taxa. Selected ethnobotanical and pharmacological sources; this is not a census. Inclusion does not establish a verified occurrence inside the watershed for every taxon.

Macroecology: regional species totals from a separate global study. The thousands shown below refer to entire botanical countries, which extend beyond the Mekong basin. They combine medicinal-use listings with native distributions; presence in a region does not establish medicinal use by its local communities.

Do not add the regional totals: species recur across countries. Dividing the atlas count by those totals would not measure completeness. The 333 botanical countries retained in the global model are also unrelated to the atlas's taxon count. Species-list expansion status.

A global analysis of medicinal plant diversity across 369 botanical countries1 identified human occupancy time as its second-most important predictor. This section reproduces the residual analysis on the published data for the seven botanical countries that contain the Mekong basin, and extends it to sub-national (province) scale.

Interpretation of the global framework

Reading the Mekong through the global framework

Six of the seven botanical countries have positive residuals classified as significant by the reproduced global model. These are whole-region patterns, not a count of medicinal plants within the watershed. The global association with human occupancy provides context; this regional reproduction does not establish a causal mechanism or local medicinal use.

Cambodia has a positive but non-significant residual. That result alone cannot establish that it is the basin's leading documentation gap. Comparisons of documentation need comparable searches and sampling effort across regions.

Internal coldspots — the sub-national extension

Exploratory documentation patterns. This province-level extension uses the limited atlas inventory, not the global medicinal-species list. Low counts can reflect source selection, collecting effort, missing records or taxonomic gaps. Coldspots are candidates for documentation review, not evidence of biological scarcity or validated fieldwork priorities. The global model's reproduction does not validate this separate extension.

At admin-1 scale: 134 provinces modelled, 4 hotspots, 2 coldspots, of which 1 combine a significant negative residual with long human occupancy and are flagged for documentation review.

Expanding species coverage

The source audit on 26 November 2026 found regional totals in the study repository and a native vascular-plant distribution file in Zenodo. Neither inspected deposit supplies the medicinal-species membership list needed for an atlas expansion.

The study used MPNS v12. Kew offers tailored MPNS data subsets; its current portal describes v15. A new version must be labelled separately from the study reproduction.

Status: awaiting a species-level medicinal list; no taxa added from regional totals. Candidates will need accepted-name reconciliation, a medicinal-source citation, and basin-presence review before inclusion. A regional native-range record alone is insufficient to confirm watershed presence.

Macroecology methods and references

Methods and reproducibility

  • Data: Shrestha et al. 2025 repository (nawalshrestha/med_plants, data.csv, reused under the paper’s DOI and commit hash recorded in the source register); WCVP (sftp.kew.org, retrieved 2026-11-25); WGSRPD level-3 boundaries (tdwg/wgsrpd); Glottolog (Zenodo record 24104033); Natural Earth 10m admin-1; GBIF occurrence API (vascular species counts per province, taxonKey=6).
  • Botanical countries: the residual analysis uses the seven WGSRPD level-3 units that intersect the basin (Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, China South-Central, China Southeast); the homepage’s “6 basin countries” refers to national boundaries, which are a different spatial unit.
  • Model: ordinary least squares, med_rich ~ vas_rich, over all botanical countries except Antarctica and Oceania (the authors’ exclusion). Significance: |residual| > 1.96 SD (the authors’ threshold in residual_histogram.R); the table reports the two-tailed normal-approximation p.
  • Extension: the same model refit at admin-1 scale inside the basin using GBIF vascular species counts per province and the atlas’s documented medicinal taxa per province. Differences from the global model are documented in the manuscript.
  • Scripts: scripts/macroecology.py, scripts/macroecology_layers.py, scripts/wcvp_match.py, scripts/harvest_admin1_vascular.py, scripts/internal_coldspots.py. Inputs cached under data/raw/shrestha2025/.
  • MPNS v12: mpns.science.kew.org was unavailable (Cloudflare 5xx) on 2026-11-25; MPNS membership badges show as unavailable and the country-level medicinal values come from the published dataset, which is MPNS-derived.

References

  1. Shrestha et al., The human fingerprint of medicinal plant species diversity, Current Biology (2025), doi:10.1016/j.cub.2025.09.050.

Harvest pressure & hydrological risk

Two operational indices of conservation pressure on the atlas taxa, built from the hydroclimatic synthesis of Tiwari et al. 2023 (Scientific Data, Zenodo 7803254, CC-BY-4.0): a harvest-pressure proxy (2050 population density × species richness × graded use score) and a hydrological-fragmentation index (share of in-basin occurrences within 10 km of a major dam). The harvest-pressure index is a proxy, not measured offtake; the hydrological index is distance-based and does not model flow alteration, sediment trapping or groundwater drawdown.

Pressure, hydrology and screening methods

Methods and reproducibility

  • Harmonization — 5 km Albers equal-area grid, 31,922 in-basin cells, per-variable resampling rules.
  • Harvest pressure — SSP2-2050 population × species richness × evidence-weighted use score.
  • Hydrological fragmentation — distance to 232 major dams, 10 km threshold.
